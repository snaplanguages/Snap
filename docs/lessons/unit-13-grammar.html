Sự Hòa Hợp Giữa Chủ Ngữ Và Động Từ

Tuyệt kỹ diệt bẫy chia động từ số ít - số nhiều, bẫy cụm bổ ngữ kẹp giữa và liên từ ghép trong đề thi TOEIC

1. Quy Tắc Chung & Bẫy Cấu Trúc Nối Bằng "AND"

Quy tắc cốt lõi: Chủ ngữ số ít / không đếm được đi với Động từ số ít; Chủ ngữ số nhiều đi với Động từ số nhiều.

Trường hợp cấu trúc "AND" Quy tắc chia Động từ Ví dụ minh họa & Giải thích
2 Danh từ riêng biệt nối bằng "AND" Chia ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ NHIỀU My sister and I like classical music.
(Cả chị tôi và tôi đều thích)
2 Danh từ cùng chỉ 1 NGƯỜI hay 1 MÓN ĂN thống nhất Chia ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT • The professor and secretary is on business. (1 người kiêm 2 chức vụ)
• Bread and meat is delicious. (Món bánh mì thịt)
• Salt and pepper is ready. (Món muối tiêu)
Phép tính cộng số học Chia ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT Two and three is five. (2 cộng 3 bằng 5)

Bẫy phân biệt 1 người vs 2 người qua mạo từ "THE"

• The professor AND secretary is... ➔ Chỉ có 1 mạo từ "The" đứng đầu ➔ 1 người đóng 2 vai vị (Giáo sư kiêm thư ký) ➔ Chia ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT.
• The professor AND THE secretary are... ➔ Có 2 mạo từ "The" riêng biệt ➔ 2 người khác nhau hoàn toàn ➔ Chia ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ NHIỀU.

2. Các Trường Hợp Luôn Chia Động Từ SỐ ÍT

Cấu trúc Chủ ngữ đặc biệt Quy tắc chia Ví dụ & Từ nhận diện
Each / Every / Many a + Danh từ số ít ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT • Each person is allowed 20kg.
• Every student has a book.
• Many a politician has promised...
Cụm V-ing / To-V / Mệnh đề danh từ làm S ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT • To jog / Jogging is good for health.
• What you have said is not true.
Tựa đề sách, phim, bài hát, tên môn học/bệnh ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT • "Tom and Jerry" is my favorite cartoon.
• Physics / Mathematics / News is hard.
• Measles / Diabetes is dangerous.
Khoảng cách, Thời gian, Tiền bạc, Đo lường ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT • Ten dollars is enough.
• Ten miles is a long distance.
• Ten years is a long time.
Tên quốc gia, thành phố kết thúc bằng "s" ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT The United States / The Philippines is powerful.
Đại từ bất định (-one, -body, -thing) ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT Everyone / Nobody knows the secret.

3. Các Trường Hợp Chia Động Từ SỐ NHIỀU & Liên Từ Cận Kề

Loại cấu trúc Chủ ngữ Quy tắc chia Ví dụ cụ thể
Danh từ tập hợp người bất quy tắc không có "s" ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ NHIỀU People / Police / Cattle / Children / Men / Women / Feet / Teeth are...
Cấu trúc: The + Tính từ (chỉ nhóm người) ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ NHIỀU The rich are not always happy.
The blind need help.
Cấu trúc liên từ ghép:
• Either A or B
• Neither A nor B
• Not only A but also B		 CHIA THEO B
(Chủ ngữ đứng sát gần động từ nhất)		 • Neither she nor her children were injured.
• Either they or their son is allowed.
• Not only she but also her friends are going.
Cấu trúc nối bổ ngữ:
S1 + as well as / with / together with / along with + S2		 CHIA THEO S1
(Chủ ngữ đứng tiên phong ở đầu câu)		 • She as well as I is going... (Chia theo She)
• Mrs. Smith together with her sons is away... (Chia theo Mrs. Smith)
MẸO THẦN CHÚ LIÊN TỪ GHÉP & BỔ NGỮ
• Gần mực thì đen: Either...or / Neither...nor / Not only...but also ➔ Chia theo Chủ ngữ B sát vách động từ!
• Đầu sóng ngọn gió: As well as / Together with / Along with / Accompanied by ➔ Lạnh lùng chia theo Chủ ngữ S1 đầu câu!

4. A Number Of vs The Number Of & Cụm Định Lượng "OF"

Cấu trúc Quy tắc Động từ Ý nghĩa & Ví dụ
A number of + N số nhiều ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ NHIỀU A number of students are going on a picnic. (Nhiều sinh viên)
The number of + N số nhiều ĐỘNG TỪ SỐ ÍT The number of days in a week is seven. (Số lượng ngày)
Some / All / Most / Majority / Half / Percent / Minority + OF + N CHIA THEO N SAU "OF" • Some of the students are late. (Chia theo students)
• Most of the water is polluted. (Chia theo water)

1. Conversation: Weekend Plan (Kế Hoạch Cuối Tuần - ID 201303)

A: Hey, what are your plans for this weekend?
B: I have no idea. Do you want to get together or do something?
A: How about going to the cinema? There are some good movies on. "Avengers" is on at Cineplex.
B: That sounds like a good idea. Maybe we should eat out beforehand.
A: It's fine with me. Where do you want to meet?
B: Let's meet at Bear's Pizza. I haven't been there for a long time.
A: Agree. I heard they just came up with a new pizza. Bear's Pizza always has the best pizza in town.
B: What time should we be there?
A: Well, the movie is on at 2:00 PM, 3:00 PM, 6:00 PM and 7:00 PM.
B: Why don't we go to the 2:00 PM show? Then, we can meet there at noon. There will be plenty of time for us to enjoy our pizza.
A: My friend Linh is in town. Can I bring her along? It's not good to leave her home alone.
B: Sure. Bring her along. It's 2 years since I last met her.

Từ vựng & Điểm ngữ pháp hòa hợp S-V trong hội thoại

• There are some good movies: "There" + are chia theo "movies" số nhiều
• "Avengers" is on: Tựa đề phim kết thúc bằng 's' vẫn chia số ít 'is'
• Bear's Pizza has: Tên cửa hàng số ít chia 'has'
• My friend Linh is: Chủ ngữ số ít chia 'is'
• get together (vp): tụ họp, gặp gỡ
• come up with (vp): nghĩ ra, sáng tạo ra

📖 Passage 1: Sexist Pricing & Gender Tax (ID 201311)

British women often have to pay more than men for almost the same things. According to a study by The Times newspaper, many products for women cost twice as much as similar things for men. The findings show that female clothes, beauty products and toys are on average 36 percent more expensive than those for men and boys. For example, buyers are charged $7 more for a pink scooter aimed at girls than for a black one. The Times refers to this phenomenon as "sexist pricing", while others call it "gender tax".

To explain for the price gap between two genders, Britain's Financial Times newspaper argues: "Perhaps women tend to be busier and have less time to shop around. Or perhaps they care more about quality..., whereas men just want something cheap." However, the Financial Times claims that men do pay more for some things and questions why journalists do not look at price differences for things that are more appealing to men.

(Adapted from breakingnewsenglish.com, 2016)

📖 Passage 2: Is There Really a Generation Gap? (ID 201314)

According to the results of a survey in USA WEEKEND Magazine, there isn't really a generation gap. The magazine's "Teens & Parents" survey shows that today's generation of young people generally gets along well with their parents and appreciates the way they're being raised. Most feel that their parents understand them, and they believe their parents consider family as the No. 1 priority in their lives. Although more than a third of teens have something in their rooms they would like to keep secret from their parents, it is usually nothing more harmful than a diary or a CD.

Such results may seem surprising in the context of the violent events that people hear about in the media. Maybe because of the things they hear, parents worry that their own kids might get out of control once they reach the teenage years. However, the facts in the survey should make us feel better. The survey shows us that today's teens are loving and sensible. They are certainly happier than the angry people in the teenage stereotypes we all know about. True, some teenagers are very angry, and we need to recognize their needs, but the great majority of teens are not like that at all.

In contrast to some stereotypes, most teens believe they must be understanding about differences among individuals. Many of them volunteer for community service with disadvantaged people. When they talk about themselves, their friends and their families, they sound positive and proud. Generally, these are very nice kids.

(Adapted from Tieng Anh 11)

📖 Passage 3: Lee Kuan Yew & Singapore (ID 201317)

Lee Kuan Yew was appointed as the first prime minister of Singapore in 1959 when the nation became a self-governing state. At that time, Singapore per capita income was only about USD400. When Lee stepped down in 1990, it was USD11,000 and in 2014 it went up to over 56,000, even higher than that of its former colonizer, Great Britain. In his memoirs published in 2000, he wrote 'They laughed at us, but I was confident that we would have the last laugh.'

Born into a middle-class Chinese family in Singapore, Lee was influenced by the language and institutions of the British rulers. He was destined to go to the UK to study law, but the Second World War broke out and he had to go to the local Raffles College where he learnt some economics. It was not until 1946 when he talked his way to Cambridge and graduated three years later with a starred first. It was during this time that he nurtured ambitions beyond starting a legal career back home.

As a prime minister, he has always been described as an efficient and inventive person. He was one of the greatest statesmen who proved to the whole world that human ingenuity, not natural resources, is the essential element of prosperity. Lee is also believed to be very forward-looking. When Singapore gained independence in 1965, he decided to keep English as the main language to increase economic benefits. Although many of his policies are still considered controversial, they helped Singapore to overcome many obstacles and become one of the most admired international business and financial centres around the world.

(Adapted from SBT Tieng Anh 12)
