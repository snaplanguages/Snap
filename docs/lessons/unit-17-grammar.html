3. Cách Cấu Tạo Trạng Từ & 16 Quy Tắc Trật Tự Từ

Cách cấu tạo Trạng từ: Adj + ly → Adv (slow → slowly, rapid → rapidly).

Lưu ý phân biệt bẫy đuôi -LY, -AL, -ING, -Y

• N + ly → Adj (likely, friendly) VS Adj + ly → Adv (quickly)

• V + al → N (arrival) VS N + al → Adj (national)

• V + ing → N (teaching) VS N + ing → Adj (boring)

• Adj + y → N (honesty) VS N + y → Adj (windy)

1. Animals (Động Vật)

A: That's a pet shop over there. Do you want to have a look inside?
B: What about Kitty? Can she come in?
A: She has to stay outside. We can tie her up here. The shop leaves some food for cat here.
B: Wow, look at all these lovely animals!
A: Yes, there are a lot of different types. I like to see them all when I come.
B: I don't even know the names of some of them.
A: Let's look at these horrible reptiles: snakes, lizards, geckos.
B: I can hear birds somewhere.
A: Yes, they are parrots and other rare birds.
B: They are all good but I like soft and fluffy animals the best.

Từ vựng & Họ từ loại (Animals)

• lovely (adj): đáng yêu (Họ từ: love (v/n))

• different (adj): khác biệt (Họ từ: differ (v), difference (n))

• horrible (adj): đáng sợ (Họ từ: horror (n))

• reptile (n): loài bò sát | lizard (n): thằn lằn | gecko (n): tắc kè | parrot (n): con vẹt

2. Sports (Thể Thao)

A: Did you watch the football match on TV last night?
B: No. I am not keen on football. What sport do you like?
A: I like playing badminton. Is it easy to play that sport?
B: Yes, it's pretty easy to play. It helps strengthen my muscles because while playing, I have to move continuously. It is good to burn calories as well.
A: Every sport brings us benefits. I like our national badminton team. Many talented players are in the team.

Từ vựng & Họ từ loại (Sports)

• continuously (adv): liên tục (Họ từ: continue (v), continuous (adj), continual (adj))

• national (adj): thuộc quốc gia (Họ từ: nation (n), international (adj))

• talented (adj): có tài năng (Họ từ: talent (n))

• strengthen (v): làm cho khỏe mạnh (Họ từ: strength (n), strong (adj))

📖 Passage 2: Zebra (ID 201714)

Each zebra has its own unique pattern of distinctive stripes, just as humans have their own unique pattern of fingerprints. Zebras stick together in herds. Within a herd, zebras tend to stay together in smaller family groups. Families are generally made up of a male, several females, and their young.

As a zebra grazes, it uses its sharper front teeth to bite the grass, and then uses its duller back teeth to crush and grind. A zebra's teeth keep growing for its entire life, because constant grazing and chewing wears them down.

Zebras are constantly on the move to find fresh grass and water. Sometimes they gather in huge herds of thousands as they migrate to better feeding grounds. They often travel in mixed herds with other grazers and browsers, such as wildebeest. Zebras groom one another. If you see two zebras standing close to each other and it looks like they're biting each other, don't worry. They're pulling loose hairs off each other as they groom. Grooming also feels good to a zebra - it's like having an itch scratched.

While most zebras are least endangered, Grevy's zebra is classified as endangered.

📖 Passage 3: Adventurers of the year (ID 201717)

THE MOUNTAINEER:
As a child, Edurne Pasaban lived in the mountainous Basque region of Spain and she climbed her first mountain when she was fourteen. THE ROAD TRIPPERS:
Steven Shoppman and Stephen Bouey were old friends who grew up together in Denver. But they knew each other a lot better after their adventure. They both had an ambition to go on a road trip round the world. From 2007 to 2010, they drove through 69 different countries during their 122,000-kilometre journey and had many adventures. They took a big risk when they went across a minefield. They also got help from lots of people and they found that the world wasn't as dangerous as they thought!
