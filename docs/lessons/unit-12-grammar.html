UNIT 12 • NEAR FUTURE (BE GOING TO)

Thì Tương Lai Gần (Be Going To)

Giải mã kế hoạch định sẵn, dự đoán có căn cứ đanh thép & Bí thuật diệt bẫy Will vs Be going to

1. Bản Chất Các Cách Dùng Của Thì Tương Lai Gần (Be Going To)

Khác với Tương lai đơn (Will) mang tính bộc phát bất ngờ, Thì Tương lai gần (Be going to) thể hiện tính chắc chắn cao và có kế hoạch/bằng chứng rõ ràng từ trước.

a. Diễn tả một KẾ HOẠCH hay DỰ ĐỊNH về một việc SẼ THỰC HIỆN trong tương lai

Hành động đã được suy nghĩ, lên lịch hoặc chuẩn bị từ trước thời điểm nói.

+ I am going to visit my grandmother in the countryside next week.
(Tuần tới tôi sẽ về quê thăm bà tôi - Dự định đã có kế hoạch từ trước.)

b. Diễn tả một DỰ ĐOÁN CHẮC CHẮN SẼ XẢY RA do có BẰNG CHỨNG trên tình huống hiện tại

Dựa vào những dấu hiệu, bằng chứng đập ngay vào mắt ở hiện tại để suy ra sự việc sắp xảy ra ngay trước mắt.

+ Look at those black clouds. It is going to rain.
(Hãy nhìn những đám mây đen kìa. Trời sắp mưa rồi - Mây đen là bằng chứng đanh thép!)

2. Hệ Thống Công Thức Chuẩn (Formulas)

(+) Khẳng định: S + am / is / are + going to + V (nguyên dạng)
(-) Phủ định: S + am / is / are + not + going to + V (nguyên dạng)
(?) Nghi vấn: Am / Is / Are + S + going to + V (nguyên dạng)?
Dạng câu Cấu trúc Ví dụ minh họa
(+) Khẳng định S + am / is / are + going to + V-inf I am going to see a film at the cinema tonight.
(-) Phủ định S + am / is / are + not + going to + V-inf I am not going to see a film at the cinema tonight.
(?) Nghi vấn Am / Is / Are + S + going to + V-inf? Are you going to see a film at the cinema tonight?

Ôn tập nhanh cách chia To Be (Am / Is / Are):

  • • Am: Dành riêng duy nhất cho chủ ngữ I (I am going to...).
  • • Is: Dùng cho Chủ ngữ số ít (He, She, It, Danh từ số ít, Danh từ không đếm được).
  • • Are: Dùng cho Chủ ngữ số nhiều (We, You, They, Danh từ số nhiều).

3. Tuyệt Chiêu Phân Biệt Tương Lai Đơn (WILL) vs Tương Lai Gần (BE GOING TO)

Tiêu chí WILL (Tương lai đơn) BE GOING TO (Tương lai gần)
1. Quyết định Quyết định bộc phát ngay tại thời điểm nói (chưa hề lên kế hoạch từ trước).
VD: The phone is ringing. I'll answer it!		 Kế hoạch, dự định đã được quyết định / lên lịch từ trước.
VD: I have bought the ticket. I am going to fly to Hue.
2. Dự đoán Dự đoán cảm tính, không có căn cứ (thường đi kèm I think, perhaps, probably, I promise, I hope...). Dự đoán chắc chắn 100% dựa vào bằng chứng hiện tại đập vào mắt (mây đen, vết nứt, mệt mỏi...).
3. Tình huống đặc biệt Dùng cho Lời hứa (promise), Lời đề nghị giúp đỡ, Lời đe dọa, Lời mời. Dùng cho Cảnh báo sự việc sắp xảy ra trước mắt (Look out! Watch out! You are going to fall!).
THẦN CHÚ DIỆT BẪY WILL vs BE GOING TO
1. Bằng chứng đanh thép (mây đen, mua vé, bụng bầu, vết nứt...) → Chọn ngay BE GOING TO!
2. Nghĩ bộc phát / Lời hứa / Suy đoán mơ hồ (I think, probably, perhaps, I promise...) → Chọn ngay WILL!
3. Thiếu chữ "TO" hay quên chia "BE" là lỗi sai kinh điển → Luôn kiểm tra đủ bộ AM/IS/ARE + GOING TO + V-INF!

1. Conversation 1: Party (ID 201203)

A: What horrible weather today. I'd love to go out, but I think it will just continue raining. Look at those black clouds!
B: Oh, you're right. It's going to rain. But the weather forecast says it will be sunny this weekend.
A: I hope so. Listen, I'm going to have a party this Saturday. Would you like to come?
B: Oh, I'd love to come. Thank you for inviting me. Who's going to come to the party?
A: Well, a number of people haven't told me yet. But, Peter and Mark are going to help us with the cooking!
B: Hey, I'll help, too!
A: Would you? That would be great!
B: I'll make some fruit salad!
A: That sounds delicious! I know my Italian cousins are going to be there. I'm sure they'll love it.
B: Italians? Maybe I'll bake a cake instead.
A: No, no. They'll love it. Is there going to be a dress code for the party?
A: No, I don't think so. Just a chance to get together and have fun. I'm sure it'll be lots of fun. But I'm going to hire a clown!
B: A clown! You're kidding me.
A: No, no. When I was a child, I always wanted a clown. Now, I'm going to have my clown at my own party.
B: I'm sure everyone will have a good laugh.
A: That's the plan!

Từ vựng & Cấu trúc diễn đạt (Party)

• going to = gonna: sắp / dự định làm gì
• horrible (adj): khủng khiếp, tồi tệ
• continue (v): tiếp tục
• thank sb for Ving/sth: cảm ơn ai vì điều gì
• help sb with sth: giúp đỡ ai việc gì
• That sounds + Adj: Nghe có vẻ...
• bake (v): nướng bánh
• instead (adv): thay vào đó
• dress code (np): trang phục quy định
• get together (vp): tụ họp, gặp mặt
• hire (v): thuê, mướn
• clown (n): chú hề

2. Conversation 2: Tourism (ID 201206)

A: Hello, I'm going to spend my holidays in a nice place. Could you offer me something interesting?
B: Yes, certainly. How long are you going to travel?
A: For about a fortnight.
B: Do you like active holidays or would you prefer to stay somewhere in a quiet place just to relax?
A: Well, I'm going to have a sightseeing tour to Europe. I think Great Britain is the best place for it. Which cities and towns would you advise me to visit?
B: I think the best way to start exploration of Great Britain is to visit London, its capital.
A: And how much is this tour?
B: Well, it depends on the hotel, but the average price is about $600.
A: And which hotel would you advise me to stay at?
B: I would recommend you The New Barbican. It's a modern hotel, comfortably furnished and the prices are quite reasonable there.
A: Is the hotel far from the main sights of the city? Where exactly is it?
B: In Central Street. It's not far from the Tower of London and St. Paul's Cathedral.
A: Fine, that will do. Let's sign a contract.

Từ vựng & Cấu trúc diễn đạt (Tourism)

• offer (v): đề nghị, gợi ý
• fortnight (n): khoảng thời gian 2 tuần (nửa tháng)
• prefer to V: thích làm gì hơn
• relax (v): nghỉ ngơi, thư giãn
• sightseeing tour (np): chuyến đi tham quan ngắm cảnh
• depend on (v): phụ thuộc vào
• average (adj): trung bình
• recommend (v): gợi ý, tiến cử
• comfortably furnished: trang bị nội thất đầy đủ tiện nghi
• reasonable (adj): hợp lý (giá cả)
• sign a contract: ký kết hợp đồng

📖 Passage 1: Trans-Siberian Railway & Highway (ID 201211)

Russia is a very large country. There are eight time zones between Moscow in the west and Vladivostok in the east. It's 9,000 kilometres and there are two ways to travel - by rail and by road.

By rail: the Trans-Siberian Railway
Trains leave Moscow almost every day. Book your tickets in advance - don't wait until you arrive in Moscow. You can book online or use a travel agent. There are two options:
Travel non-stop in seven days. You sleep and eat on the train. You can talk to other passengers, learn some words in Russian and enjoy the views. The train travels through amazing mountains, beautiful forests and strange deserts.
Stop on the way and stay in hotels. Go sightseeing in the big cities. In Novosibirsk - the main city in Siberia - there are museums, art galleries, theatres and a famous opera house in the city centre. Or visit the Kungur Ice Cave near Perm. From the towns of Irkutsk or Ulan-Ude, you can take a bus or train to Lake Baikal, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Lake Baikal is 636 kilometres long and there are only four or five towns near it. The lake is a great place for sports activities - diving, hiking and horse riding are all popular.

By road: the Trans-Siberian Highway
Are you adventurous? Then take the new Trans-Siberian Highway. Drive your car or - for the trip of a lifetime - hitch-hike with Russian drivers in their cars and lorries. And when you finally arrive in Vladivostok, you can fly home or continue your trip - there's a boat to Japan every week.

(Adapted from Life)

📖 Passage 2: Emma's Birthday Party (ID 201214)

Emma is going to have a birthday party next week. She is turning four. Her parents and her sister is going to the party early to set up. At first, they thought about having a party in the garden or at home, but there are going to be a lot of people, so they decided to have it at the park. One good thing about the park is they have a lot of tables. They also have a covered pavilion in case it rains. Emma is planning on getting wet anyway, because they have slides and they have a water sprinkler area for people to run through.

Last year, they also had a birthday party there. They covered tables with pink and red tablecloths. They put out napkins, cups, forks and plates. Then they put out the food, but left it covered until later. Her dad made sure there was enough ice with drinks. It was going to be hot, so people needed cold drinks. Her mom set out some activities, like coloring and small games before people came. There were mini water guns too and even a giant cardboard castle for people to color with crayons and markers. It was really great.

Everyone started to show up. Gifts in pretty bags and boxes started to pile up on the table. Kids played with a football, with the games and with water guns. Everyone ate joyfully. There were sandwiches, chicken fingers, veggies with dip, chips, soda and lots of things! After they ate, it was time for a piñata. The kids took turns smacking the piñata with a stick until it broke. Emma took the first turn, and other boys and girls tried. When candy and toys fell out, everyone grabbed as many as they could.

Finally, it was time to sing a popular song "Happy Birthday" and eat cupcakes. Emma made a wish, blew out the candles and took a big bite of a cupcake. Everyone cheered and ate their own cupcakes while she opened the presents. She got a lot of nice toys and clothes, so she was very happy. This year, she is going to have a greater party with a lot of friends coming. She is looking forward to the party.

(Adapted from Havefunteaching)

📖 Passage 3: Underground Paris (ID 201217)

The great avenues are quiet; the shops are closed. There's the smell of fresh bread from a bakery somewhere. It would be hard to say which time of the day in Paris I prefer but this is probably it. Soon the streets will be full of people and traffic. As with most other cities, you see the real Paris when the city wakes up.

There is, however, another part of Paris which is silent and free from people 24 hours a day. Under the city are hundreds of kilometres of tunnels. There are sewers and old subways but there are also spaces of all kinds: canals and catacombs, wine cellars which have been made into nightclubs and galleries. During the 19th century, the Parisians needed more stone for buildings above the ground so they dug tunnels beneath the city. After that, many farmers grew mushrooms in them. During World War II, the French Resistance fighters also used them. During the 1970s, many groups of young people spend days and nights below the city in these tunnels. It's a place for parties, theatre performances, art galleries - anything goes here!

Everywhere you go under Paris, there is history and legend. Historians and novelists often refer to them in their books. For example, Victor Hugo mentions the tunnels in his famous novel Les Miserables and in the story and musical The Phantom of the Opera there is a pond beneath the old opera house. Most people think this is myth but in fact there is an underground pond here with fish. A 'normal' tourist can visit parts of Paris beneath the ground. For example, there are the catacombs beneath the Montparnasse district. Here you can see the bones and skeletons of about six million Parisians. The bodies came from cemeteries above the ground two centuries ago when the city needed more space.

However, it's illegal to enter other parts of the tunnels and police often search the area. It's also very dangerous because some of the tunnels might collapse. Nevertheless, there are people who will take you to visit them. I have found two 'unofficial' tour guides - Dominique and Yopie (not their real names). They take me through many tunnels and after a couple of hours we arrive at a room which isn't on any map. Yopie and some of his friends built it. The room is comfortable and clean with a table and chairs and a bed. Yopie tells me there are many other places like this. 'Many people come down here to party, some people to paint ... We do what we want here.'

(Adapted from Life)
