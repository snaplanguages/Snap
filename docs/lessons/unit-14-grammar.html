1. Quy Tắc Vàng: OSASCOMP

Khi dùng từ 2 tính từ trở lên để miêu tả cho một danh từ, nếu các tính từ cùng loại thì phân cách bằng dấu phẩy; nếu khác loại thì sắp xếp theo đúng thứ tự OSASCOMP:

OSASCOMP = Opinion - Size - Age - Shape - Color - Origin - Material - Purpose
Ký tự Loại tính từ Bản chất nghĩa Ví dụ minh họa
O Opinion (Ý kiến) Nhận xét, đánh giá cá nhân useful, beautiful, interesting, lovely, delicious, handsome, glorious, luxurious...
S Size (Kích cỡ) Kích thước to nhỏ, độ dài ngắn big, small, large, huge, tiny, long, short, tall...
A Age (Tuổi tác) Tuổi, độ mới cũ, thời kỳ old, young, new, brand-new, ancient, modern...
S Shape (Hình dáng) Kiểu dáng, hình học round, triangle, cubic, heart-shaped, flat, square, oval...
C Color (Màu sắc) Màu sắc bề ngoài black, red, white, yellow, cream, violet, navy blue, emerald...
O Origin (Nguồn gốc) Xuất xứ, quốc gia, vùng miền Vietnamese, English, Indian, Thai, German, American, French, Japanese...
M Material (Chất liệu) Vật liệu cấu thành silk, gold, silver, wooden, metal, plastic, leather, glass, concrete...
P Purpose (Mục đích) Mục đích sử dụng (thường là V-ing/N) sitting, sleeping, wedding, waiting, racing, sports...
📌 Ví dụ phân tích thực tế:
• a beautiful (Opinion) old (Age) Indian (Origin) lamp → Một chiếc đèn Ấn Độ cổ tuyệt đẹp.
• a luxurious (Opinion) big (Size) new (Age) red (Color) Japanese (Origin) car → Một chiếc ô tô Nhật Bản to mới màu đỏ sang trọng.
MẸO THẦN CHÚ "Ông Sáu Ăn Súp Cua Ở Má Pín"
Hãy ghép các chữ cái đầu tiên: Opision (Ông) → Size (Sáu) → Age (Ăn) → Shape (Súp) → Color (Cua) → Origin (Ở) → Material (Má) → Purpose (Pín)!
⚡ Nhớ khẩu quyết: Cảm nhận cá nhân (Opinion) đứng đầu tiên, Mục đích sử dụng (Purpose) sát đít Danh từ!

1. Conversation 1: People Around You (ID 201402)

A: Do you know Peter, Jane?
B: Yes. He is my neighbor.
A: Really? How is he?
B: Well, he's a friendly man. He always greets me when we meet.
A: I think so because sometimes I talk to him. He keeps smiling.
B: He lives in a big brick house. (big: size | brick: material)
A: It must be very nice.
B: Sure. It's a new 2-floored red house with a yellow flower roof.
A: Great. I must see it if I have a chance to be near there. Does he have any siblings?
B: Yes. He has an older sister. She's gorgeous. Everyone says she is an attractive slim woman. (attractive: opinion | slim: shape) She has got long blonde hair with big brown eyes.
A: He looks like his sister, doesn't he?
B: Yes. All members in his family have got curly blonde hair. We have a good relationship.
📚 Từ vựng cốt lõi & Trật tự tính từ trong bài:
• Keep + Ving: không ngừng làm gì | brick (n): viên gạch
• gorgeous (adj) / attractive (adj): quyến rũ, thu hút | slim (adj): mảnh khảnh | blonde (adj): màu vàng hoe
• big brick house: size - material | attractive slim woman: opinion - shape | big brown eyes: size - color

2. Conversation 2: Wedding (ID 201406)

A: Have you ever attended someone's wedding? Who was he/she?
B: Yes, a lot. But my most memorable one is my best friend.
A: What do you think about it?
B: It was a pretty well-organized wedding. It took place at a famous luxurious big restaurant downtown.
A: How about the food?
B: I still remember that there were some delicious French dishes. I'd never tried them before.
A: Were the bride and groom nice?
B: Definitely. The bride, my best friend, looked brightly and the groom was so gentle.
A: They are a lovely couple, aren't they?
B: They are the happiest couple I've ever known. Exchanging rings is the ritual I'm quite interested in. That's when the groom in a nice black suit and tie and the bride in a fantastic white wedding gown officially become husband and wife.
📚 Từ vựng cốt lõi & Trật tự tính từ trong bài:
• attend (v): tham dự | well-organized (adj): tổ chức tốt | luxurious (adj): sang trọng | groom (n): chú rể | bride (n): cô dâu | wedding gown (np): váy cưới
• famous luxurious big restaurant: opinion - opinion - size
• delicious French dishes: opinion - origin | fantastic white wedding gown: opinion - color - purpose

📖 Passage 1: Pancake Day or Shrove Tuesday (ID 201410)

Pancake Day or Shrove Tuesday is a special day celebrated in many countries around the world. It is celebrated in English-speaking countries like the UK, Ireland, Australia and Canada. In some countries, like France and the USA, it is called "Mardi Gras" or "Fat Tuesday". In other countries, like Spain, Italy or Brazil, Shrove Tuesday is at the end of Carnival. On this day many people eat pancakes, a thin, flat cake made in a pan.

Pancake Day is always on a Tuesday in February or March. It is the day before Ash Wednesday, the start of Lent. Lent is a period of 40 days before Easter when people often give up or stop eating things that are bad for them like chocolate or fast food. At the end of Lent is Easter, which takes place on a different date each year because it depends on the moon. Traditionally, during Lent, people didn't eat rich foods like butter and eggs, so they made pancakes from these ingredients on Shrove Tuesday.

Another tradition on Pancake Day in the UK is pancake racing. People run in a race with a pancake in a pan. As they run, they have to toss the pancake (throw the pancake in the air and catch it in the pan) several times. In some pancake races people dress up in fancy dress costumes. The most famous pancake race takes place in a town called Olney, in the middle of England. People say that Olney has been celebrating pancake races since 1445!

(Adapted from British Council)

📖 Passage 2: Volunteering Work in Britain (ID 201411)

Doing some forms of voluntary work has never been more popular with British people. Over 20 million people were engaged in voluntary activities in 2013. Volunteering means giving up time to do work of benefit to the community. It can take many forms, from working with children with learning difficulties, or in an animal hospital, or planting trees. When London won its bid to host the 2012 Olympics, up to 70,000 volunteers were needed to help ensure that the games were a success.

Volunteers can be anyone of any age. Students and full-time workers often manage to involve in some voluntary work. But what motivates volunteers? Some do it for a sense of selflessness while others find they have free time available. Many mention the opportunity to get to know people they would not normally meet.

A relatively new phenomenon is the hope of meeting new friends or even a life partner through volunteering. In a recent British survey, 20% of 18-24 year-olds and 8% of over-65s said their love lives had improved since they began volunteering. The same poll found that nearly half of volunteers enjoyed improved health and fitness, a quarter lost weight - especially those working with children or doing conservation projects - and two-thirds felt less stressed. So, it seems volunteering may improve your life, and you may even find the person of your dreams.

(Adapted from "Volunteering - for Love" by Magus, 2013)

📖 Passage 3: Effects of Television on Family Life (ID 201413)

Many people worry about the effects of television on family life in the United States. In the average home, the TV is on more than six hours a day. But do people always keep their mind on the programs? And do people who watch TV really spend less time on other free-time activities?

A study by some New York researchers found out that watching TV is the most popular free-time activity in the United States. More than 70% of Americans say that they watch TV every day, or almost every day. The second most popular activity is reading the newspaper. Listening to music at home is third. Talking on the phone to friends and relatives is fourth. Exercising or jogging is fifth.

Surprisingly, television does not seem to take time away from free-time activities at home. Six out of ten people said that when the TV is on, they seldom pay attention to it. During a typical television program, people may eat dinner, do housework, read a newspaper or magazine, or talk or read to their children. The TV becomes 'background music'.

The researchers learned that in some ways Americans are very traditional. Teenagers and single people may be interested in activities and excitement outside the home. However, eight out of ten Americans like to spend their free time at home with family member and friends.

(Adapted from "Reading Explorations")
